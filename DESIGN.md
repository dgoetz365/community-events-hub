# Design system

This Operate surface uses a community roster's quiet visual rhythm: forest-green shell, warm paper ground, compact system type, and linked schedules as the signature. The navigation and controls remain standard web UI. The brief authorizes direct implementation; no browser review was performed.

## Tokens

- Ink `#223932`; secondary text `#52655c`; primary action/shell `#214d3c`.
- Ground `#f8f9f3`; work surface `#ffffff`; secondary surface `#e8eee4`; rules `#cbd4c8`.
- Attention text `#962f25`; attention surface `#fff1ed`; keyboard focus `#185cc0`.
- System sans-serif throughout; body 16px, records 14px, H1 32px, H2 20px.
- Space in 4/8/12/16/24/32/40px steps. Standard 5px corner radius; minimum 44px controls.

## Interaction

Events and members have dedicated directories. A row link opens the record; an explicit Review appearance link opens the member/event junction. The event and member always remain reachable through visible links. An appearance shows status, capacity and conflict reasons before its actions. Problems are announced in a live region and remain on screen. Save failures retain the entered values for correction; Refresh records explicitly reloads saved values.

## Responsive behavior

At 1000px the detail form drops below the linked record list. At 680px the navigation becomes a wrapping horizontal bar and forms stack. Large record tables scroll within their own region rather than forcing page overflow. Motion is limited to native interactions and a one-pixel pressed-button state, removed under reduced-motion preference.

## Direction record

The grounded candidates were a repair manual, community noticeboard, workshop register, print edition, garden-society roster, zine listings and museum program. The assigned fifth direction supplies the forest palette and linked roster rhythm. The airport challenger contributes explicit wayfinding discipline; the dense-web and convention-catalog challengers contribute compact records; the dance-score challenger contributes readable time alignment; the reference-setting challenger contributes consistent rule spacing. They do not replace familiar navigation or turn the UI into a physical prop. The source detector is a mechanical check only; it is not visual approval.
